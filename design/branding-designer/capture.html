01The callRead this first. Everything after is the evidence.

Build Branding as a document builder, not a canvas. A brand pack is a sequence of pages. Each page is a stack of section rows in one fixed grammar: a number and a title in column one, the paragraph in column two, the content block beneath. The designer registers three colour systems (Brand, Supporting, Neutral), a typography hierarchy and an image set as data; the pages draw them. Every artefact carries a Why record, and the app writes the decision log as the last section and renders the PDF itself.

Why it wins on value. Nobody in the market does three of our four things: a colour defined in Lab and proofed to a named press condition, a structured reason behind every artefact, and a print-ready PDF with bleed and an output intent. The fourth, a document you own outright rather than rent monthly, is the Colorgain business model already. Each of those is a line in the sales page, and none of them is a feature Frontify or Canva can add in a quarter.

The one real trade-off. A fixed page grammar gives up free placement. Designers who want to drag a swatch to any pixel will feel the rails. The alternative, a free canvas, gives up the thing that makes the output credible: every page in the pack looking like it came from the same hand, and the PDF paginating itself. I recommend the grammar, with one free-form block (text, image, quote, on a 12-column grid) so the brief and the story pages still breathe. If a client later needs a loose layout, that is Web & Screen or Print, not Branding.

What Rick decides. Section 08 lists the five open questions. The rest is ready to be built from the artboards.

02What Herman Miller taught usbrandstandards.hermanmiller.com, read in full: 21 pages, 56 colour assets, 12 type styles. Images were not reachable; structure and every value were.

The order of the book

Rationale comes before any rule. Pages 2 to 7 are belief, who we are, what we do, how we show up, voice and history. Accessibility sits at page 8, before a single visual rule. Then identity, colour, typography, photography, illustration, graphic elements, infographics, composition, motion, video, applications, in use, resources. The framing line on page one is worth keeping on our wall: "the definitive source of truth, a rubric by which we gauge the success of everything we make… guidelines are just that, a guide. Always use your best judgment."

The row grammar, which the artboards copy

Every sub-section is the same row: a hairline across the top, the number and a bold title in the first column, the explanatory paragraph in the second, an action on the right where there is one, and the content block beneath, starting at the paragraph's left edge. Rick's screenshots of 10.2, 10.3, 10.5, 10.6, 11.1, 11.2 and 11.3 all follow it. It is the single most useful thing on the site: one grammar, dozens of content blocks.

  • Roles before swatches. One hero colour, then a core set, then an extended set of fifteen. That maps directly onto Brand, Supporting and Neutral.
  • Two value sets, shown separately. RGB and HEX for digital, CMYK and Pantone for print, with the line "Digital and print applications require colors built in different blend spaces." The data behind the page also stores Pantone uncoated and RAL, but never shows them. No Lab anywhere.
  • Tints and shades are not brand colours. They exist only in an infographics palette (Blue 01, Blue, Blue 02) and are forbidden in brand material. Our tint ladder stays a tool for proofing and UI, not a page of the pack, unless the designer registers it.
  • Use proportion is a chart, not a sentence. A single bar: Red at 30 percent, fourteen supporting values at 5 percent each. We draw the same bar at 60 / 30 / 10 by system.
  • Pairing is shown, never described. A grid of each colour with a black or white dot, then a full matrix of combinations to avoid, then a sparse matrix of the fully AA-compliant pairs. Every one of those is computable, so we generate them.
  • Contrast is stated with the size rule attached. "White text on Red is unacceptable below 19px." Large text at 19 px bold or 24 px needs 3:1, normal text needs 4.5:1, graphics 3:1.

Typography, section 11, twenty sub-sections

History · Primary typeface · Weights in use · Leading · Tracking · Hierarchy · Scale and proportion · Capitalization · Punctuation · Whitespace · Line length · Margins · Principles · Grids · Application · Alternates · Primary alternate · Secondary alternate · Additional languages · Additional don'ts.

  • The specimen is the hero. 11.1 is the family name set enormous, then the alphabet. 11.2 is a row per weight: the weight's name on the left, upper case, lower case and numerals on the right, a hairline between rows. Our Typography artboard does exactly this.
  • Leading and tracking are multipliers, not sizes. Headline 0.98, Subhead 1.00, Detail 1.12, Body 1.34 times the size. Tracking −25 for headlines, −5 subhead, 0 body and detail. And the scale is relative: "select a base type size in a composition and make all other sizes proportionate", with X, X×1.5, X×6, X×10, X×18.5 as the example.
  • Hierarchy by weight, named as voices. "Söhne Kräftig is our primary voice… Halbfett and Buch are both supporting voices." Three weights only, each with an italic.
  • Typesetting rules are short and absolute. Sentence case. Always left-aligned, never justified or centred. 45 to 75 characters a line. Type no wider than three quarters of the page. Fallbacks named: Arial for system apps, Inter for Google apps, Noto for CJK.

What works poorly, and we avoid

  • The whole book ships as one 5.8 MB page. Ours is a file the client owns, paginated.
  • The data is dirty: CMYK stored as strings in places, duplicate names, core versus extended not explicit in the record. Our colour is one typed record with the system as a field.
  • Contrast badges are static and the site admits "examples shown may not be inclusive of web standards." Ours are computed from Lab at export time.
  • Typography gives no point sizes at all. Elegant, but an implementer has nothing to type. We store both the ratio and the resolved sizes.

Palette and type tools worth knowing

  • Leonardo (Adobe) builds tint scales from target contrast ratios against a declared background, not from picked colours, and exports CSS variables and tokens. Adobe rebuilt Spectrum with it.
  • Radix gives every one of twelve steps a job: 1 to 2 backgrounds, 3 to 5 component states, 6 to 8 borders, 9 to 10 solid fills, 11 to 12 text, with guaranteed text pairs.
  • Material 3 defines tonal palettes that vary in tone only, with Primary, Secondary, Tertiary, Neutral, Neutral Variant and Error as the roles, and a 15-style type scale of Display, Headline, Title, Body, Label at three sizes each.
  • Coolors, Adobe Color, Huemint, Khroma are exploration tools. None builds a system with roles, proofs or reasons; they are where our Library's palettes get sampled, not competitors.

The gaps, which are our product

  • Rationale as data. No platform stores a structured why per colour, font or image and renders it as a narrative. Decision records exist for software (Nygard's ADR: title, status, context, decision, consequences), not for identity work.
  • Colour-managed values. Every tool is hex-centric. A Lab master with an ICC-proofed CMYK and a named press condition is not offered anywhere found.
  • A real print PDF with bleed, trim and an output intent. Web-first tools treat PDF as a download button, Corebook as the enemy.
  • Ownership. The market charges per editor per month. A local app that produces a client-owned file has no direct competitor in what was found.

What you can put on a page, and how the page is shown.

  • Right: three house checkboxes, Why marks, Grid, Bleed; then the Template dropdown (Colorgain Swiss · A4 landscape).
  • Reasoning: the bar already holds filters and toggles elsewhere in the app. Insertion belongs here because it is a verb on the page, not a property of the document.

Viewport-Page

A page-thumbnail column on the left, the current page at trim size in the middle, bleed and trim marks drawn around it.

  • Every artefact on the page is a block from the registered data. Select one and it gets the orange ring, the house focus colour, and the Why panel follows it.
  • The small ink tags, W07 and so on, are Why marks. They print in the PDF when the checkbox is on, so the client can find the reason behind any element in the log.
  • Thumbnails carry the section number, the name and a status dot: filled agreed, half proposed, empty untouched.

Viewport-Footer, 42 pt

How the page is viewed and what the values mean.

  • Left: page navigation and zoom.
  • Right: the value toggles (HEX, RGB, CMYK, Lab, Pantone off until licensed), the proof condition, and Export PDF as the one ink button. Menus open upward, as the footer's do now.
  • Reasoning: this mirrors the Library footer, where format, size, Delta and profile already live. A designer moving between Library and Branding finds the same things in the same place.

Rail2 · Document

The outline. Sections with page counts and the same status dots, a free-form page or a section added from here, and the one switch that writes the decision log as the last section.

Rail2 · Colour Systems

Three blocks, Brand, Supporting, Neutral. Each shows its strip, its source palette from the Library, its roles, and its proof status against the current press condition, in the same words Delta Bloom uses. Register a system is the only button.

  • Reasoning: registration is the act that makes a Library palette a brand asset. The page cannot draw a colour that is not registered, which is the governance Frontify sells.

Rail2 · Typography

Families and allowed weights, the fallback stack and the licence note, then the scale: base, ratio and the level table with weight, tracking, size and leading. Add a level and Specimen on page are the actions.

Rail2 · Imagery

The mix bar by category, the image list with resolution and gamut at the placed size, the three or four rules in words, and the on-brand / off-brand pairing action.

Rail2 · Why

The decision record for whatever is selected. Status as a house choice control (Proposed, Agreed, Superseded), decision in one line, the why in a box, alternatives struck through with their reason, evidence computed live, who and when, Save to log and Show on page.

  • Reasoning: this is the differentiator, so it is a panel of its own and never a modal. It can be moved to Rail1 like any panel.

Rail2 · Output

Four presets (print book, screen presentation, web guidelines, asset pack), the include switches, the press condition and intent, a pre-flight list of findings, and the lead button with the page count in it.

Where it lives and what comes out

  • A brand pack is an asset of the member it belongs to, in the member's folder: proposed as .colbrd, plain JSON like every other asset, pointing at the registered .colpal and .coltyp by id, holding the pages, the Why records and the output settings. The images stay as files beside it. Name and extension are Rick's to settle, question 03 below.
  • Outputs: PDF/X-4 with bleed, trim marks and the output intent embedded for print; an sRGB 16:9 PDF with the why text as presenter notes for the client meeting; static HTML for the web; and an asset pack with .colpal, .coltyp, ASE, JSON tokens, PNG swatches and the log as Markdown.
  • Pantone, RAL and NCS names appear only when the user holds the licence, per the third-party colour rules already agreed. The toggle exists in the footer and is off.
  • Press profiles come from ColorSync's installed list, so the same code serves the Store build.
06Why it worksThe reasoning against the five laws and the product's promise.
  • It is the product's promise made visible. A client sees a green defined in Lab, proofed to FOGRA51 and shown with a ΔE, beside the sentence that explains why it was chosen. That is enterprise colour management in a leaflet-sized brand, which is the pitch.
  • The grammar does the design work. Because every section is the same row, a family nursery with no designer produces a book that reads like Herman Miller's. The template carries the Swiss laws: baseline justification, balance, fixed boxes for changing text, so nothing beneath a rewritten paragraph moves.
  • Reasons compound. The log is written from the records, so the narrative is never out of date, and a superseded decision is proof of rigour rather than an embarrassment. Agencies will buy this to show their process; clients will buy it to remember theirs.
  • It reuses what exists. Palettes and typography palettes from the Library, Delta Bloom's proof language and settings, the panel primitive, the house controls, the footer's value and profile menus. Branding adds page templates, the Why record and a PDF writer; it does not add a second colour engine.
  • It sells twice. The PDF is the client deliverable; the asset pack is the developer and printer deliverable. One file, two audiences, no monthly fee.
07What the artboards do not show yetDeliberately left for the next round, so the first round stays reviewable.
  • Pages 5 and 6, Pairs and Tints and proofs: the generated pairing grids, the AA matrix and the contrast-targeted tint ladder. The panels and the page list already name them.
  • The cover and the Applications page: a leaflet, a label and a van side drawn from the registered data.
  • Template choice beyond Colorgain Swiss · A4 landscape: Letter, A5 portrait and 16:9 are the obvious three.
  • The client's approval flow: a second name on an Agreed record, and a Draft watermark until it is there.
  • Measurement: the screenshots must be measured against the real window once built, per the house rule.
08Five decisions for RickEach with the recommendation first.
Page grammar or free canvas?

Recommend the grammar with one free-form block type, as argued in 01. Web & Screen and Print stay the free canvases.

What is the panel called: Why, Rationale or Decision?

Recommend Why. It is the word a client uses, it is three letters on a page tag, and the log page can still be titled Decisions.

The file: one .colbrd asset, or pages inside the member's .colinf information pack?

Recommend its own asset. A pack is shared and exported on its own, and the information pack should stay the member's notes, not a 12-page document.

Default paper: A4 landscape or Letter?

Recommend A4 landscape, matching the catalogue's European press conditions, with Letter one dropdown away and the Store build following the region.

Is Branding a Pro feature in the Store edition?

Recommend yes for the PDF/X-4 and asset-pack outputs, no for building and the screen PDF. The book is the thing people pay for; the builder is how they find out.
