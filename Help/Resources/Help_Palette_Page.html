Palette Page

Use the Palette Page to explore a library of palettes, open a palette’s individual colours, organise your choices and predict how those colours will translate to a delivery target. The same page supports screen design, photography, print, video and rendering workflows.

You do not need to understand colour science before starting. Begin with the short walkthrough, then use On This Page to reach a specific control or the professional detail you need. The Help topics on the far left take you to other guides. The contents panel highlights the section you are reading.

Start With One Palette

  1. Open a catalogue in Library, then choose a palette list or a palette in its tree. The centre page shows the selected location. Open a palette to see its colours separately.
  2. Choose the delivery workflow and target in the profile header. For a normal web delivery, select a screen workflow and sRGB. For print, use the actual printing condition supplied by the printer.
  3. Read the target values and colour differences. Turn on Delta View in the page footer to compare the displayed colour with its predicted target appearance.
  4. Inspect a colour in Histograms or Delta Bloom. A warning is a reason to investigate the stated condition, not an instruction to change the master immediately.
  5. Save a named preset if you need to repeat those settings. Rate or flag palettes to organise your review independently of the proofing result.
Viewing is not rewriting. Selecting a target, changing a tolerance or switching a view does not replace the saved master colours. Rename, editing, copying, assigning and deleting are separate actions with their own effects.

Palettes, Library And Ownership

A palette is a named, ordered set of colour entries. Each entry refers to a colour definition. The colour definition preserves source information and a device-independent master; the page calculates target representations from that master. A palette name or its place in the tree does not define its colour space.

The Library provides navigation. Your own palettes and favourites give convenient ways to find work, while Folders follows the active catalogue’s configured hierarchy. That hierarchy can use different names and levels in different catalogues. Navigate by the names your catalogue supplies, rather than assuming every tree has the same structure.

  • Your palette list shows your available app-owned palettes according to the current ownership and filtering rules.
  • A catalogue folder or asset location is an owning destination. A palette assigned there belongs to that location and is found through the catalogue tree.
  • A favourite is a shortcut to an existing palette. Removing the shortcut does not delete the palette.
  • A copied palette is independent of the original. A copy into another owning destination retains the source; later edits to the copy should not be mistaken for edits to that source.

Use the destination review when copying or assigning a palette. Read the action label before committing: copying, moving and deleting have different consequences. If a destination contains several possible asset locations, expand the tree or destination chooser until the intended location is unambiguous. See Library and Folders for navigation and recovery.

A view filter can hide a palette without removing it. Changing catalogues changes the library context. If something seems missing, check the active catalogue, owning location and filters before importing another copy.

Find Your Way Around The Page

The profile header sits between the rails. It describes the active viewing setup. Below it, the page tab identifies the open subject. A palette-list page contains palette cards; an individual palette page contains its colour swatches. The title and counts identify which level you are viewing.

The actionbar holds the available page filters and its help hexagon. Rating and flag filters narrow a palette list; ordering changes how the matching palettes are arranged. Filter by Meta opens the metadata filter controls. A filtered result is a view of the underlying library, not a new palette or a new owning folder.

Use the tree to change the selected location and the page tabs to return to open views. The plus and split controls belong to the panel where they appear. A new panel offers navigation choices. Opening a second panel gives another view; it does not duplicate an asset file.

Read Cards And Swatches

A palette card shows a strip of its colours, its name, its colour count and review controls. Open the palette to inspect the entries individually. A swatch shows its name and the available value for the selected target. Read the colour-space or target label with the numbers: identical numbers in different colour spaces do not necessarily describe the same colour.

Hex describes an RGB encoding, not a universal physical colour. CMYK percentages describe an ink combination under a particular printing condition. RGB values for video depend on the encoding and signal range. A linear rendering value can exceed 1 or fall below 0; this does not automatically mean the master definition is corrupt.

Δ on a swatch indicates a predicted colour difference. Δmax on a palette card is the largest available predicted difference among its colours, not their average. An unavailable prediction must be investigated separately. A small maximum among available results does not prove that unavailable colours passed.

The Halo action mark opens contextual actions for that palette or colour. It is an action menu, not a proofing verdict. Read the separate Halo guide for its controls, destinations and confirmation behaviour.

Preset: A Saved Viewing Setup

Preset recalls a named setup. It is different from an ICC profile: a preset can include the workflow, target, rendering intent, black point compensation, signal range and tolerance. An ICC profile describes a colour condition used by a conversion. A preset can reference such a profile without containing or installing that ICC file.

Open Preset to see your custom saved setups first, followed by the grouped built-in target choices. Select a saved name to apply it. Use the inline edit control to open its settings, export to share its saved setup, or remove to remove that saved preset. Removing a preset does not delete palette colours or uninstall an ICC profile.

The sliders button beside the header controls opens the adaptive editor. Enter a meaningful name and use Save New to keep a separate setup. Update replaces a selected saved setup with the current settings. Rename changes its name; Duplicate makes an independent preset. Import and Export exchange the app’s .colpro setup files. The editor may expose these actions alongside the saved list or on its rows.

Changing a header setting makes a current setup that may differ from the saved preset. Save or update deliberately if you want to recall that change later. A useful name describes the intended delivery, such as “Uncoated print review” or “Rec. 709 full range”. Avoid a name that hides a different target or tolerance.

The viewing setup drives the page previews and the shared analysis panels. Splitting the page does not create a separate proofing policy for each split. Local controls such as chart ordering or Grid/List affect their own presentation.

Workflow: Choose The Kind Of Delivery

Workflow narrows the targets and reveals settings that matter to the selected kind of output. It does not change the saved library hierarchy.

WorkflowWhat to look for
Design And PhotographyScreen and photographic RGB spaces, supported RGB ICC profiles and appropriate monochrome encodings.
PrintA real CMYK or supported grey printing condition, rendering intent and black point compensation.
Video And CinemaThe required display encoding, cinema target or HDR target, plus signal-range settings where applicable.
3D And VFXSupported scene-linear and grading encodings, including ACES-related choices. Confirm the downstream pipeline’s exact requirements.

Changing Workflow updates the available Target options. Recheck Target and Tolerance after switching. A workflow category is a navigation aid, not a certificate that a particular delivery specification has been met.

Target: Where The Colour Must Go

Target answers “Which output condition are we predicting?” Choose the exact requirement, not simply the largest-looking gamut.

  • Screen and web: sRGB is a common web interchange target. Display P3 describes a different gamut and encoding. Adobe RGB and ProPhoto RGB serve different photographic workflows. Use the target required by the receiving system.
  • Print: select the printer, process and paper condition that the supplier specifies. A named FOGRA or GRACoL condition and Generic CMYK are not interchangeable. Generic CMYK is not evidence of a particular press result.
  • Video: distinguish Rec. 709, Rec. 2020 and HDR encodings such as PQ or HLG. Primaries, transfer characteristics and reference luminance all matter. “RGB” alone is insufficient delivery information.
  • Rendering: distinguish scene-linear working encodings from display encodings. ACEScg, ACES2065-1 and ACEScct serve different pipeline roles. A target conversion is not a complete scene rendering or creative grading pipeline.

For applicable video targets, Full Range and Legal Range choose the code-value range. Full-range RGB should not be judged against a restricted legal-range band. Keep the app setting consistent with the destination application, renderer or encoder.

Installed and linked ICC files supply actual conversion data. A familiar profile name does not make a missing file available. Missing profiles need to be located or installed from a trusted publisher; a saved preset cannot substitute for the file. The ICC Profiles guide explains linked files and recovery.

Master Colours in the target catalogue returns the page’s master/source presentation. Read it as a reference view, rather than a simulation of a particular press or encoded delivery. To investigate a delivery difference, select a concrete target and check the header again.

Rendering Intent And Print Settings

The profile editor shows the extra controls relevant to the target. These settings can change both the predicted colour and the difference reported against its master.

SettingMeaning
Relative ColorimetricMaps source and destination whites relative to each other. Colours outside the destination gamut must be mapped to reproducible values.
Absolute ColorimetricRetains the destination medium’s white relationship for proofing. A simulated paper white can make whites look different from the screen background.
PerceptualUses the profile’s perceptual mapping to preserve useful relationships across a gamut change. Colours already within gamut can also move.
Black Point CompensationAccounts for differing source and destination black points where supported. It is not an instruction to add black ink or a control for the K percentage alone.

The available intents and their results depend on the transform and profile. A disabled option is not silently promising the same effect through another path. Paper-white and K-only choices, where offered, are specialised simulations. Recheck their state before comparing two setups.

For production work, agree the target and rendering policy with the recipient. An attractive screen preview cannot replace a suitable output profile, controlled viewing conditions or a measured production check.

Tolerance And Colour Difference

A colour difference asks how far a predicted target colour is from its master. Smaller generally means closer under that calculation. Tolerance is the limit you choose for the review. A colour within the limit receives the passing glyph; one above it receives a warning. This evaluates the active prediction, not every possible output process.

For example, with a ΔE00 tolerance of 2.0, a calculated difference of 0.7 is within tolerance, while 3.4 is above it. Increasing the tolerance to 4.0 changes the verdict on 3.4. It does not improve the colour or change what the target can reproduce.

The app uses ΔE00 for ordinary output comparisons and ΔE ITP for supported HDR comparisons. These are different metrics. Keep the metric visible with the value and threshold; do not assume 2.0 means the same perceptual difference under both. A numerical limit is a review policy, not a universal boundary between visible and invisible.

Reported differences are predictions from the colour definitions and the selected conversion. They are not spectrophotometer measurements of a printed sheet. To evaluate physical output, use a measured workflow with stated measurement and viewing conditions.

Unavailable is a third outcome. If a transform or required ICC file is unavailable, there is no valid proofing result for that colour. A dash, retained master display or missing target value must not be read as ΔE zero or as a pass.

Delta View: Compare Without Replacing

The Delta switch in the viewport footer turns the split comparison on or off. With comparison enabled and a valid prediction available, the colour block retains its base presentation in the upper half and shows the selected target prediction in the lower half. On a palette strip, the comparison applies to each colour position.

Keep the selected target in mind when interpreting the base presentation and the numerical captions. The comparison is a viewing aid, while the captions identify the available target values. Turning Delta Off hides the split comparison; it does not reset the target, erase the saved master or turn off the analysis calculations.

A palette’s Halo may offer a local Print preview toggle. This can override that tile’s comparison display without changing the global target. If one card appears different from its neighbours, check the local preview state before assuming its colours have changed.

A subtle visual change can still produce a meaningful numerical difference, and a monitor can conceal an output difference it cannot display. Use the numbers, selected metric and target identity together. The page background also changes your visual impression without changing the underlying colour.

Read Histograms And Delta Bloom Together

Both panels follow the active viewing proof, but they answer different questions.

  • Histograms · Channel Levels shows how target component values are distributed. Merged and individual-channel views help inspect values. A channel advisory concerns a stated band or range, such as an applicable video signal range or a print channel guard band.
  • Delta Bloom · Output Difference compares predicted output colours with their masters. Its petal values and result glyphs use the active difference metric and tolerance. The summary counts available results within tolerance and separately identifies unavailable results.

A bright neutral can be inside the selected colour-difference tolerance while triggering a channel advisory. That is not contradictory: one result concerns colour difference, the other concerns component levels. Similarly, a colour can sit inside component bands yet have a large target difference.

Inspect a selected colour and read its values or explanatory tooltip. Expand opens the panel’s larger view. These panels guide investigation; they do not automatically edit or approve a colour. See Histograms and Delta Bloom for their dedicated controls.

Ratings, Flags, Favourites And Picker Targets

These controls organise palettes. They are separate from the numerical proofing result.

FlagA saved review marker. Assign whatever review convention your team agrees. A flag is not a warning glyph or an automatic technical approval.
FavouriteAdds a shortcut to Favs. It keeps the same palette identity and ownership.

Open Halo actions for rename, duplicate, destination and sharing choices. Read the selected palette’s name before any action. Ratings and flags belong to the palette and should not be confused with an unsaved view filter.

Footer Controls And Theme

The viewport footer belongs to the page you are viewing.

  • Grid arranges matching palettes or colours as tiles for visual comparison.
  • List gives a row-oriented presentation of the same underlying items. Switching the view does not move palettes between owning locations.
  • Zoom slider changes the tile or presentation size where applicable. It does not change colour precision, the master definition or a target conversion. Its effect can be reduced in List view.
  • Delta switch controls the split target comparison described above. It is not the tolerance selector.

The app footer is separate. Its five theme selectors sit to the left of the build/release label, ending at the left edge of the right rail. They change the viewing background and the app’s readable text treatment. They do not convert your palettes or choose an output profile.

Try a neutral background when comparing close colours, and avoid judging colour fidelity only against one extreme background. The copied-colour history and palette carousel are navigation or copy aids; they do not define where a palette is owned.

Tabs, Splits And Saved Layouts

Use a panel’s split controls to compare views side by side or one above the other. Drag tabs to rearrange views. The trailing close control closes the panel; it disappears when only the final panel remains. Closing a view does not delete its palette.

Layout in the app header recalls saved arrangements. A catalogue page layout stores reusable page splits; an app layout stores a broader workspace arrangement. A layout is not the same as a colour preset. After loading either one, check the visible target, selected palette and filters before resuming a review.

A Repeatable Review Routine

  1. Define the destination. Ask which screen encoding, printing condition, video specification or rendering space the recipient requires.
  2. Open the correct owning location. Confirm the active catalogue and palette names. Clear unintended rating, flag or metadata filters.
  3. Set up the prediction. Choose Workflow and Target, then inspect rendering intent, black point compensation and signal range where relevant.
  4. Choose the review policy. Set Tolerance with the metric visible. Use a threshold agreed for this task, rather than treating the default as a universal production standard.
  5. Inspect the evidence. Read individual differences, unavailable results and channel advisories separately. Use Delta View and the larger analysis panels when useful.
  6. Record your organisation choices. Rate, flag or favourite palettes without mistaking those markers for measured approval.
  7. Save and communicate. Save the preset with a clear name. When handing off values, include the target identity and relevant conversion settings. A screenshot of a colour alone is insufficient specification.

Terms At A Glance

Master
The device-independent colour reference used for comparisons, retained separately from target values.
Gamut
The range of colours a space or output condition can represent.
ICC profile
Characterisation and transform information for a colour condition, not a palette or a saved UI layout.
Preset
A saved viewing-proof setup that recalls related choices together.
Target
The space or output condition used for the prediction.
Rendering intent
The policy used by a supported profile transform when mapping between colour conditions.
ΔE
A colour-difference value. Its formula and conditions must be known before comparing results.
Tolerance
The selected limit used to judge the current difference calculation.
Channel advisory
A finding about component levels or a stated range, distinct from a colour-difference verdict.
